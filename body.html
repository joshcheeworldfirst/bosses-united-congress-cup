▮ Background

A team sheet of company directors

Bosses United Singapore was established in 2022 by members of the Bosses Network Young Chapter (BNYC). It was never meant to be another amateur social side. The founding idea was simple: business owners rarely get an excuse to be in the same place every week with a shared objective, so build one.

What the team gives its members is a standing appointment — a reason to stay active, form friendships that aren't transactional, trade knowledge between businesses, and put something back into the community. Their own framing is that success isn't confined to office walls; it can also be built on grass and sweat.

The football has become genuinely serious. From casual kickabouts the squad moved to organised, competitive fixtures, with real gains in tactics, fitness and the chemistry that only comes from playing together across several seasons. Spokesperson Albert Ng speaks for the team publicly.

▮ Anticipated Q&A

Questions the CEO may be asked

Eight things a member, a journalist or the Minister’s office could reasonably ask on Wednesday — and the answer that holds up.

How many members does Bosses United actually have?

150+ members and counting. That is the number to say from the stage. The 44 companies profiled elsewhere on this page are the Singapore businesses with a public, verifiable trading profile — the recognisable tip, not the total membership. Never present 44 (or 47, which includes a duplicate and two non-commercial associations) as the size of the community. 41 of the 44 profiled reach $1M turnover or more.

What is this community worth commercially?

Aggregate estimated turnover is $226M–$586M, midpoint around $406M. It is heavily concentrated: the 13 distinct Tier 0 companies account for $191M–$476M — roughly four-fifths of the value in under a third of the names. These are estimates for sizing, not filed accounts, so avoid quoting a single precise figure.

Why football? Isn’t this just a sponsorship?

No. Every player is an owner or director, so the pitch is a room full of decision-makers who meet weekly by choice. The team exists so that business owners have a standing reason to be in the same place with a shared goal. We are not buying attention — we are joining something that already convenes our exact customer.

What has WorldFirst actually done with them so far?

Four touchpoints since 13 July: the ‘Dear You’ client screening at Golden Village with over a hundred guests, a friendly match at SAFRA covered by three photographers, the launch ceremony on 7 August, and the interview series on 21 August. Three more land this week, then monthly clinics from October.

Where is the cross-border business in this?

Sixteen member companies have documented cross-border activity — rice imported from nine or more source countries, 4,241 recorded import shipments at TEHC, Rigel operating subsidiaries in China, Malaysia, Indonesia and Vietnam, furniture imported from Vietnam, steel stock, and workforce recruitment from Bangladesh, India and Thailand. That is evidenced FX and payments demand, not an assumption.

Why are six markets represented at a Singapore event?

Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Japan, Korea and Hong Kong. Five of the six already have a thread back to someone on the member list — exports to Malaysia, recruitment from Thailand, a Japanese franchise, and the Hong Kong MOU plus a Bosses United Hong Kong chapter. Korea is the exception with no existing member link, so treat it as a forward-looking corridor rather than claiming a connection.

What is the relationship with Hong Kong?

Bosses United signed an MOU with the Hong Kong Amateur Football Association after touring Hong Kong in April 2025. It creates cross-border fixtures, but was written deliberately wider than football — giving HKAFA access to Singapore’s football-business ecosystem and Bosses United a route into Hong Kong’s entrepreneurial network. Their own framing: a foundation, not a conclusion. Credit it as their initiative, not ours.

What are we asking of them next?

The Q4 clinics: one session a month from October to December, capped at 20 people each — up to 60 curated seats. Position the cap as selectivity rather than a constraint, and use Wednesday to make attendance feel earned.

▮ For the briefing

Talking points

Five things the CEO can say that show we know exactly who we're standing next to.

Every player is our customer

Not a sponsorship at arm's length. The community runs to 150+ owners and directors, and the 44 Singapore businesses we can name already show $1M+ turnover in 41 of them. The audience is the target market, standing in kit.

“A hundred and fifty business owners, and we’ve only put names to forty-four of them so far.”

Qiaopi to WorldFirst

From the 13 July screening. The remittance letters in Dear You are the ancestor of what we do — an earned link between the film we hosted and the company hosting it, and a warmer opening than a product line.

“We watched a film about qiaopi together in July. We've been in that business ever since — just faster.”

They crossed the border first

The HKAFA MOU was their initiative, not ours, and explicitly about more than fixtures. Acknowledging it signals we read past the football to the business intent.

“You signed Hong Kong before we ever brought it up. We'd like to be useful for what comes after the MOU.”

Thirty years of community

Credit the lineage, not just the team: The Bosses Network since 1996, BNYC since 2015, the squad since 2022. The older dates land better with the founders in the room than praise for the football.

“The team is four years old. The community behind it is thirty.”

Name the trade, not the product

Sixteen member companies have documented cross-border activity — rice from nine source countries, steel stock, Vietnamese furniture, subsidiaries in four ASEAN markets. Naming a specific flow beats naming a WorldFirst feature.

“Rice from nine countries, furniture from Vietnam, washroom tech in ten markets. That's our day job.”

The programme already has a name

The July screening banner read The Road to WorldFirst — International Payments Made Simple. Using that phrase on Wednesday makes seven weeks of separate events read as one deliberate campaign rather than a series of favours.

“We called it the Road to WorldFirst in July. Wednesday is the part of the road where we hand something over.”

Welcome him back, not for the first time

MoS Dinesh Vasu Dash was at the 7 August launch and receives the ceremonial presentation there on record. Wednesday is his second appearance with this partnership inside three weeks — the welcome should read that way.

“Minister, thank you for being with us again — twice in three weeks is its own kind of endorsement.”

Use their words, not ours

The team bus carries their own tagline: “Football is our passion. Business is our mission.” Quoting it back is the single cheapest way to show the CEO knows this community rather than having been briefed on it an hour earlier.

“Football is your passion, business is your mission. The second half of that sentence is where we come in.”

Close on the clinics

The Q4 clinics are the ask: three sessions, 20 people each, up to 60 curated seats. Wednesday is the right moment to make attendance feel selective rather than solicited.

“Twenty seats a month from October. We'd rather have the right twenty than a full room.”
